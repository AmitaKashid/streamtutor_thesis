# Voice latency investigation

## Current behavior and limits

The browser receives revisable microphone hypotheses by re-running a recognizer on a growing audio snapshot; these calls are not a native streaming STT protocol. The first microphone activation asynchronously loads and warms a separate local, CPU `tiny.en` faster-whisper model with beam size 1 for previews. Its initial download may take time and needs internet access. The server logs `[LIVE PREVIEW] Ready` once it is available. Until then, and if initialization fails, the configured STT backend provides previews. Set `LLMTUTOR_PREVIEW_MODEL=off` before starting the backend to disable the additional model, or set a different compatible faster-whisper model name. The configured STT backend remains authoritative for the final transcript. A slow inference cannot show new words until it returns, and short utterances may still finish before the first preview. Preview text is provisional and can change; the smaller model may be less accurate and adds CPU and memory load. No student audio is sent to the model download service.

The preview starts once 500 ms of audio is available, including pre-roll, to avoid using the sole preview slot for almost entirely silent input. The browser resampler carries fractional sample positions across blocks so it does not lose audio at block boundaries. The preview retains quiet frames after VAD detects speech, and does not enqueue repeated stage notifications on every 20 ms frame. At endpointing, pending preview results are invalidated and final STT takes priority over a preview that has not entered inference. A GPU call already in progress cannot be preempted. The shared Whisper model uses a process-wide lock for both STT and alignment; HTTP STT backends use a separate session lock. Different GPU models can still contend for GPU resources.

Normal utterances now end after 800 ms of VAD silence, down from 1000 ms. This reduces the fixed end-of-speech wait but may split a speaker's pause; assess this on real speech. During tutor playback, interruption detection requires eight consecutive 20 ms WebRTC VAD frames, each with RMS at least 300; regular microphone capture retains its previous threshold. Sustained voice pauses playback and recognition decides whether to cancel or resume it. Louder thresholds can miss quiet interruptions, while echo or background speech can still trigger pauses or inaccurate recognition. The VAD reset clears the actual segment and pre-roll buffers. The first browser speech segment can end at a shorter sentence/comma boundary and uses a smaller character target. This may reduce time to start synthesizing and aligning, but can change prosody or introduce gaps; measure rather than assume improvement.

The known video sign-off hallucination is filtered in the final and preview transcripts, including curly apostrophes. Phrase-based filters can also reject a student intentionally saying the same phrase; they cannot detect every hallucination.

Qwen emits audio without word timestamps. Browser audio continues to wait for each complete speech segment and Whisper forced alignment, preserving the word-synchronization approach. This is not first-packet streaming, and segment synthesis and alignment are still on the first-audio critical path. Later segment synthesis can overlap earlier alignment with a bounded queue, and ready browser buffers are scheduled ahead. GPU contention or late segments can still cause gaps. Unsupported word alignment falls back to showing the segment after its audio with a warning. Timestamps are estimates. A genuinely low-latency and accurate alternative would require an incremental aligner or synthesis service with word timing, plus validation against real audio; there is no verified zero-latency solution in this change.

## Metrics and interpretation

The displayed `STT latency` is final STT plus model-lock wait; it excludes the user's speech and endpoint silence. `First audio` begins at the backend pipeline timer after STT and ends just before the first audio is encoded and sent, not when a speaker produces sound. `Post-STT total` was previously labeled `Total pipeline`; it excludes capture, endpointing and final browser playback. `TTS first chunk` includes synthesis and alignment after the first TTS text segment is ready, up to the same pre-send point. LLM TTFT is time to the first answer delta, including thinking when enabled. Thinking is measured from the first observed reasoning delta to the first answer delta. `Last reasoning → answer` measures the last observed reasoning delta to the first answer delta; it can include network and client buffering and is not an independently observable exact end-of-thinking event. These metrics overlap and must not be added blindly. Missing values should remain missing; interrupted or failed turns are not complete comparisons.

The restored pie chart shows only the separate measured STT, answer TTFT, and TTS preparation intervals, not a breakdown of total response time. Endpoint silence, retrieval, text accumulation before the first TTS segment, and other gaps are omitted. Thinking and first-audio timings remain in the list but are excluded from the pie because they overlap the slices.

For optional timing logs, set `LLMTUTOR_LATENCY_DIAGNOSTICS=1` in the environment **before starting the backend**. `[PARTIAL STT TIMING]`, `[FINAL STT TIMING]` and `[ALIGNMENT TIMING]` report audio duration, lock wait, and inference wall time without printing transcript text. Qwen server logs provide synthesis timing. Browser developer tools show `[AUDIO UNDERRUN]` when a segment starts more than 50 ms after the previous scheduled end. `[WORD ALIGNMENT WARNING]`, `[TUTOR PIPELINE ERROR]`, and `[BARGE-IN]` distinguish some failure and interruption paths. These logs do not measure device playback or front-end display latency.

## Acceptance and measurement

Use the same hardware, selected STT/TTS/LLM, prompt, RAG and thinking settings before and after the change. Record cold and warmed runs separately. Compare several repeated typed turns and spoken turns, TTS on and off, short and long questions, and thinking on and off. Record the time between speaking and each visible preview, silence endpoint delay, final transcription time, answer TTFT, first audio emitted and audible onset (using loopback or manual timing), word-onset error, and audio gaps. Test speech with low volume, ordinary pauses and short questions, and voice interruption while alignment is active. Record recognition errors and sentence/prosody quality as well as latency. A paused browser or background timer throttling can affect visual synchronization.

The unit tests simulate clocks, VAD and model calls. They do not establish latency, STT accuracy, GPU memory usage, timing accuracy or perceived speech quality on a real device.
